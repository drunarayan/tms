Magic of Exponentials¶

🌾 Module 1: The ancient Indian story of the Chaturanga¶

Mathematical Concept: Sum of a Finite Geometric Series
Core Formula - looks scary - but we will understand it soon!
$(S_{n}=\frac{a(1-r^{n})}{1-r}=a(2^{n}-1)\quad (\text{when\ }r=2))$

1.1 The Legend of Chaturanga¶

In ancient India, the inventor of chess (Chaturanga) presented his game to the ruling king. As a reward, the inventor requested a seemingly trivial prize:

  • 1 grain of rice on the first square of the board.
  • 2 grains on the second square, 4 grains on the third, 8 grains on the fourth, and so on.
  • The payout must double continuously for each of the 64 squares on the board.
    The king immediately accepted, failing to realize that geometric series grow flatly at first but quickly reach an unimaginable compounding explosion on the back half of the board.

[Square 1: 1 grain] -> [Square 2: 2 grains] -> [Square 3: 4 grains] ... -> [Square 64: 2^63 grains]

1.2 Mathematical Derivation & Scale¶

To calculate the total obligation of the king, we extract our inputs:

  • Initial term (a) = 1
  • Common ratio (r) = 2
  • Total terms (n) = 64
    $ (S_{64}=1\times (2^{64}-1)=\mathbf{18,446,744,073,709,551,615}\text{\ grains}) $

1.3 Reality Check vs. Indian Rice Production¶

To put this massive abstract integer into a physical perspective, we translate the grains into standard agricultural metric weight:

  • Average Weight of a Single Rice Grain: ≈ 0.025 grams (2.5 × 10⁻⁵ kg)
  • Total Metric Mass Calculation: $ M=18.4467\times 10^{18}\times 2.5\times 10^{-5}\text{\ kg}=4.61168\times 10^{14}\text{\ kg}=\mathbf{461,168,601,842}\text{\ Metric\ Tons} $ According to modern agricultural records, India produces roughly 150.18 million metric tons of rice annually. By dividing our results, we find:
    $ \text{Years\ of\ Production\ Required}=\frac{461,168,601,842\text{\ Tons}}{150,180,000\text{\ Tons/Year}}\approx \mathbf{3,070.77}\text{\ Years} $
    The king unknowingly promised the inventor over 3,000 years of modern India's entire collective rice production.

🦠 Module 2: Viral Transmission & Epidemic Curves¶

Mathematical Concept: Unchecked Discrete Exponential Scaling
Core Formula:
$N(t)=N_{0}\times 2^{\frac{t}{D}}$

2.1 The Mechanics of a Doubling Period¶

During early viral transitions like the outbreak stages of COVID-19, public health specialists rely on tracking the doubling time (D) to map hospital capacity limits. When an infectious pathogen lands in an immunologically naive population with zero restrictions or vaccines, its expansion mirrors a perfect compounding geometric progression.

2.2 Applied Population Scenario¶

Assume a viral variant hits a dense metropolitan region with an inherent doubling window of exactly 3 days (D = 3). A super-spreader event sets off a baseline community load of 100 initial active cases (N₀ = 100).

  • Predictive Equation: $N(t) = 100 \times 2^{\frac{t}{3}}$
  • 30-Day Evaluation Window (t = 30):$\text{Total\ Doubling\ Intervals\ }(n)=\frac{30\text{\ Days}}{3\text{\ Days}}=10\text{\ Cycles}$$N(30)=100\times 2^{10}=100\times 1,024=\mathbf{102,400}\text{\ Active\ Cases}$

2.3 The Healthcare Takeaway¶

Exponential growth curves are deceptively flat during the early stages. For the first 12 days, regional case numbers crawl gently from 100 to 1,600, which might make local officials feel secure.
However, in the final three days of the month alone (from Day 27 to Day 30), the case pool surges instantly from 51,200 to 102,400. This rapid doubling can instantly break regional ICU infrastructure, highlighting the absolute mathematical necessity of swift interventions to flatten the curve before it hits the vertical explosion phase.

🏥 Module 3: Radiopharmaceutical Elimination & Half-Life¶

Mathematical Concept: Continuous Exponential Decay via Combined Half-Lives
Core Formula:
$A(t)=A_{0}\times \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{t}{T_{e}}}$

3.1 Nuclear Diagnostic Clearance¶

Technetium-99m ($^{99\text{m}}\text{Tc}$) is a radioactive tracer injected into patients to analyze heart blood flow (myocardial perfusion). To ensure safety, medical tracers must vanish quickly from the human system through two simultaneous exponential channels: physical decay and biological clearing.

  1. Physical Half-Life ($T_{p}$): Natural physical isotope decay rate = 6 Hours
  2. Biological Half-Life ($T_{b}$): The rate at which organs naturally excrete the element = 24 Hours

3.2 Finding the Effective Half-Life ($T_{e}$)¶

To track the real-time disappearance of radiation inside a patient, we combine both half-lives using a harmonic equation:
$\frac{1}{T_{e}}=\frac{1}{T_{p}}+\frac{1}{T_{b}}\implies T_{e}=\frac{T_{p}\times T_{b}}{T_{p}+T_{b}}$
$T_{e}=\frac{6\times 24}{6+24}=\frac{144}{30}=\mathbf{4.8}\text{\ Hours}$

3.3 Tracking Precision Mass Decay (Picogram Scale)¶

A standard diagnostic dose for cardiac monitoring is 740 Megabecquerels (MBq). Due to its short half-life, this strong dose of radiation requires a microscopic initial physical mass of only 38 picograms (3.8 × 10⁻¹¹ grams). Let's calculate the tiny mass remaining after 24 hours.

  • Find the Elapsed Decay Intervals (n) after 24 hours:$n=\frac{24\text{\ Hours}}{4.8\text{\ Hours}}=\mathbf{5}\text{\ Complete\ Cycles}$
  • Compute Remaining Mass (M₂₄):$M_{24}=M_{0}\times \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{5}=38\times \left(\frac{1}{32}\right)$$M_{24}=\mathbf{1.1875\times 10}^{\mathbf{-12}}\text{\ grams}\quad (\mathbf{1.1875}\text{\ Picograms})$
    After one day, less than 1.2 picograms of the active tracer remain inside the human body, demonstrating how exponential decay keeps nuclear medicine procedures highly safe for diagnostics.

📝 Part 5: Homework Worksheet & Analytical Challenge¶

Problem Statement¶

A retail saver places a principal starting sum of ₹1,000 into a compound portfolio that yields an average annual return of 7%. This balance is left completely untouched to grow for a time horizon of 60 years.
Complete the tasks below to calculate the terminal value and find the tracking error of standard mental estimation shortcuts.

Task 1: Complete the Exact Compound Balance Calculation¶

Using the standard continuous periodic compounding formula:
$A=P(1+r)^{t}$

  • Inputs: P = 1,000, r = 0.07, t = 60
  • Math Execution:$A_{\text{exact}}=1,000\times (1+0.07)^{60}=1,000\times (1.07)^{60}$$A_{\text{exact}}=1,000\times 57.94643197=$ ₹ $\mathbf{57,946.43}$

Task 2: Calculate the Mental Approximation via the Rule of 72¶

Use the Rule of 72 to estimate your results without complex exponents:

  1. Find the Doubling Window:$\text{Estimated\ Doubling\ Time\ }(D)=\frac{72}{\text{Interest\ Rate\ Percentage}}=\frac{72}{7}\approx \mathbf{10.2857}\text{\ Years}$
  2. Determine Total Compounding Cycles (n) across 60 Years:$n=\frac{60\text{\ Years}}{10.2857\text{\ Years}}\approx \mathbf{5.83333}\text{\ Cycles}$
  3. Compute the Rule of 72 Terminal Value:$A_{\text{rule72}}=1,000\times 2^{5.83333}=1,000\times 57.017518=$ ₹ $\mathbf{57,017.52}$

Task 3: Calculate the Formula Discrepancy & Structural Error %¶

Find the exact variance percentage between your precise result and the mental shortcut:
$\text{Error\ }\%=\frac{|A_{\text{exact}}-A_{\text{rule72}}|}{A_{\text{exact}}}\times 100$
$\text{Error\ }\%=\frac{|57,946.43-57,017.52|}{57,946.43}\times 100=\frac{928.91}{57,946.43}\times 100=\mathbf{1.6030\%}$

Analytical Conclusion¶

The Rule of 72 provides a highly dependable mental baseline, holding to a tiny 1.60% deviation even when stretched over a multi-generational, 60-year investment track. The shortcut is highly accurate here because a 7% asset return matches the exact mathematical baseline where logarithmic scales linearize perfectly near the number 72.

import numpy as np  
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt  
  
# 1. Initialize time array for a standard 30-day month  
days = np.arange(1, 31)  
  
# 2. Option A: ₹1 Lakh (₹100,000) received every single day for 30 days  
daily_lakh = 100000  
total_lakh_track = days * daily_lakh  
  
# 3. Option B: 10 Paise (₹0.10) that doubles each day for 30 days  
# Day 1 = ₹0.10, Day 2 = ₹0.20, Day 3 = ₹0.40... Payout on Day n = 0.10 * 2^(n-1)  
daily_doubling_payouts = 0.10 * (2 ** (days - 1))  
total_doubling_track = np.cumsum(daily_doubling_payouts)  
  
# 4. Print key mathematical milestones to the terminal for the class  
print("--- Final Verified Lesson Milestones ---")  
print(f"Day 10 -> Option A (Linear): ₹{total_lakh_track[9]:,d}   | Option B (Exponential): ₹{total_doubling_track[9]:,.2f}")  
print(f"Day 20 -> Option A (Linear): ₹{total_lakh_track[19]:,d} | Option B (Exponential): ₹{total_doubling_track[19]:,.2f}")  
print(f"Day 24 -> Option A (Linear): ₹{total_lakh_track[23]:,d} | Option B (Exponential): ₹{total_doubling_track[23]:,.2f}")  
print(f"Day 25 -> Option A (Linear): ₹{total_lakh_track[24]:,d} | Option B (Exponential): ₹{total_doubling_track[24]:,.2f}")  
print(f"Day 30 -> Option A (Linear): ₹{total_lakh_track[29]:,d} | Option B (Exponential): ₹{total_doubling_track[29]:,.2f}\n")  
  
# 5. Build and render the visualization plot  
plt.figure(figsize=(11, 6))  
plt.plot(days, total_lakh_track, color='#dc2626', marker='o', linewidth=2, label="Option A: ₹1 Lakh/Day (Linear)")  
plt.plot(days, total_doubling_track, color='#16a34a', marker='s', linewidth=2.5, label="Option B: 10 Paise Doubling (Exponential)")  
  
# Enhancing visual layouts and text overlays  
plt.title("Linear Accumulation (₹1 Lakh/Day) vs. Exponential Doubling (10 Paise Start)", fontsize=13, fontweight='bold')  
plt.xlabel("Day of the Month", fontsize=11)  
plt.ylabel("Total Accumulated Fortune (Indian Rupees - ₹)", fontsize=11)  
plt.xticks(days)  
plt.grid(True, linestyle="--", alpha=0.5)  
  
# Clear integer formatting for the Y-axis numbers to display crores clearly  
plt.ticklabel_format(style='plain', axis='y')  
plt.gca().get_yaxis().set_major_formatter(plt.FuncFormatter(lambda x, loc: "{:,}".format(int(x))))  
  
plt.legend(fontsize=11)

plt.show()
